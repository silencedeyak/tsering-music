Featured Work

Eight-Act Mythological Dance Drama · 1983–1985

“Sai·Dun Yue and Dun Zhu”

A monumental theatrical achievement, this large-scale mythological dance drama represents the first complete score for such a production composed independently by a Tibetan musician. The work weaves traditional Tibetan melodic motifs with sophisticated orchestration, creating an emotional and narrative journey through ancient Tibetan mythology.

Premiered to critical acclaim in Beijing (1987), it earned the Ministry of Culture's Outstanding Literary and Artistic Creation Award and established Tsering as a composer capable of sustaining complex dramatic narratives through music.

Musical Inspirations

Where the material comes from

🏔️

Tibetan Plateau Landscapes

The vast, sacred geography of Tibet — from snow-capped peaks to endless grasslands — provides the spatial and emotional foundation for his compositions.

🙏

Monastic Chant Traditions

The deep vocal traditions of Tibetan Buddhist monasteries, particularly from Labrang Monastery, inform the harmonic and melodic structures of his work.

🎶

Folk Song Heritage

Traditional Tibetan folk songs — work songs, love songs, ceremonial songs — form the melodic DNA running through all of his compositions.

🎻

Western Classical Tradition

Training in Western harmony, counterpoint and orchestration allows him to present Tibetan musical ideas with sophisticated structural complexity.

🎭

Theatrical Storytelling

Tibetan opera (Lhamo) and Reba performance traditions influence his approach to musical narrative and dramatic pacing.

💫

Spiritual Philosophy

Buddhist concepts of impermanence, compassion and interconnection shape the emotional and philosophical content of his musical expression.
